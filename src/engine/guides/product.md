Product photography — full guide (commercial, e-commerce and brand imagery; the product is always the hero)

1. Product sheet first (one identity)
- Before writing any prompt, look at the product photo(s) and write one short product sheet: object type, shape and proportions, materials and finish (matte, gloss, brushed metal, frosted glass), exact colors (hex when clear), label layout, logo position, the legible text on the pack.
- Repeat that sheet word for word in every step prompt. Never restyle, recolor or redesign the product unless asked.
- Pass the product photo as a reference in every step (refs: the asset id); later steps also reference the approved hero shot.
- Pack text: quote it literally in quotes and only when legible in the photo; never invent claims, logos or labels.

2. Light (always direction and temperature)
- Name key, fill and rim/back light: position (45° left, top, behind), quality (large softbox, strip light, hard sun, diffused window) and color temperature in K (5600K neutral daylight, 3200K warm tungsten).
- Studio: soft key + white bounce fill + rim light to separate the edges. Luxury: low-key, one hard key, deep shadows, specular highlights. Lifestyle: motivated natural light that matches the scene.
- Glass and liquids: backlight or strip lights for edge definition. Metal and gloss: large soft sources, controlled reflections, no hot spots on the logo.

3. Lens and aperture per shot
- Hero / packshot: 85–100mm, f/8–f/11, whole product sharp, eye level or slightly above.
- Macro / detail: 100mm macro, f/2.8–f/5.6, shallow focus on the material, finish or mechanism.
- Lifestyle / with hands: 35–50mm, f/2.8–f/4, product in focus, background softly blurred.
- Flat lay: top-down 90°, 50mm, even light.

4. Surface, set and shadow
- Name the surface (seamless white sweep, stone, oak, linen, acrylic riser) and the contact shadow (soft grounded shadow, subtle reflection). A floating product without a shadow looks fake unless the concept asks for it.
- Props only if they support the story (ingredients, use context); never compete with the product in color or size.

5. Destination sets the frame
- Marketplace / catalog: 1:1, pure or near-white background, product fills ~80% of the frame.
- Instagram feed: 4:5. Stories, Reels, TikTok: 9:16 with safe margins (top and bottom 15% free). Pinterest: 2:3. Web banner or email header: 16:9 or wider, product on one third, negative space on the other side for copy.
- Leave clean negative space where copy will go and say where ("empty space on the right third").

6. Brand
- Brand colors in hex in every prompt that uses them (background, props, light gels). One palette for the whole set.

7. Formats (variants of the workflow)
- Packshot: neutral or white background, several clean angles. Lifestyle: real environment, the product in use, hands or action. Close-up with a person: hands, face and product, application or demo. Pinterest: vertical moodboard aesthetic. Hero banner: wide, copy space. Carousel: 3–10 slides with one visual system (same light, palette and surface; each slide one idea). Ad pack: one master adapted to each platform ratio. Try-on: a generated model wearing or using the product. Conceptual / CGI: levitation, splashes, sculptural or surreal sets for premium DTC, fragrance, beauty and tech. Restyle: change the look, mood or season of an existing image and keep the subject and composition.

8. Features shot (infographic)
- Generate a clean shot with deliberate empty areas for callouts. Never bake long text, lists or specs into the image: callouts are added afterwards as text layers in the Designer.

9. Second pass (optional, only if the user said yes)
- An edit step on the chosen result: fix label text and logo fidelity, clean edges, remove artifacts, even out reflections; the instruction names only what to fix and says to keep everything else.
- Video (optional, only if the user said yes): animate the approved hero: slow 360° turntable, gentle push-in or orbit, soft moving reflections; the product never deforms.

10. Avoid
- Empty modifiers ("8k, masterpiece, ultra detailed"). Several light setups in one prompt. Changing the product between shots. Busy backgrounds behind a catalog shot. Text the photo does not show.
