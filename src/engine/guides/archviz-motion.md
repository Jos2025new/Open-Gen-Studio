Architectural walkthroughs and flythroughs — video from an approved render

1. The render is the anchor
- Every clip starts from an approved still (first_frame) of the same building; the prompt never redescribes the architecture, it directs the camera and the life in the scene. Mention the building as its @Name when it is a saved place.
- One camera move per clip, slow and stable; 5–10 s per clip (long single moves up to the model's maximum only when asked).

2. Camera language (pick one per clip)
- Exterior: slow dolly-in toward the entrance; lateral tracking along the facade; slow orbit around the corner (30–60°); drone flyover rising to reveal the site; pull-back reveal from a detail to the whole house; low-angle push past foreground planting.
- Interior: smooth dolly forward through the living room; slow pan from the kitchen to the window wall; gentle crane down from double height; walk-through at eye level (1.5 m).
- Transition exterior → interior: dolly through the open glazed door into the hall, in one continuous move, or two clips joined (end of the exterior clip near the door, interior clip starts inside).
- Say the speed and the end framing: "slow, steady, ends framing the pool terrace".

3. Life in the scene (subtle)
- Leaves and grasses moving in a light breeze, soft moving sun shadows, ripples and reflections on the pool and glass, curtains moving slightly, one or two people walking at scale, warm interior lights turning on at dusk.

4. Keep the structure stable
- Write: "architecture stays rigid and unchanged; straight verticals; no warping, no new openings". The camera never passes through walls or glass unless it goes through an open door.

5. Several clips (a tour)
- Order: arrival / facade → entrance → main interior → private space → terrace or garden → closing wide shot.
- Neighboring clips change shot size or angle; each starts from its own key still of the same building (refs: the hero render), then all are joined in order. Ambient music under the whole tour when asked.

6. Avoid
- Fast or shaky moves, zoom-ins that blur detail, spinning orbits, rooms that change layout mid-clip, people as the subject, text overlays.
