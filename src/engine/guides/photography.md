Photography glossary — shots, lenses and light for any still (portrait, scene, social, editorial). Optional reference.

1. Shot and angle (name the end framing)
- Extreme wide / establishing: the whole place, subject small. Wide: full body with surroundings. Medium: waist up. Close-up: face or object fills the frame. Macro: texture-level detail.
- Eye level: neutral. Low angle: looking up, power, scale. High angle: looking down, vulnerability. Bird's-eye / top-down: flat lays, maps, patterns. Dutch angle: tilted horizon, unease (use rarely). Over-the-shoulder / POV: viewer inside the scene.

2. Lens (focal length changes perspective, not just framing)
- 14–24mm wide: environment, exaggerated depth, distortion near edges; never for close faces.
- 35mm: documentary, street, subject in context. 50mm: natural, close to the eye.
- 85mm: portrait, flattering face, soft background (f/1.8–2.8). 135–200mm telephoto: compression, background pulled close, isolated subject.
- 100mm macro: tiny detail. Tilt-shift: miniature look. Fisheye: extreme curve (stylized only).
- Depth of field in words: "shallow depth of field, background softly blurred" (f/1.4–2.8) or "everything sharp" (f/8–11).

3. Light recipes (source, direction, quality, color)
- Golden hour: low warm sun from the side or behind, long shadows, honey glow, soft rim on hair.
- Blue hour: cool ambient sky, warm practical lights, calm.
- Overcast / open shade: soft even light, low contrast, true colors (good for skin and products).
- Window light: one soft side source, gentle falloff into shadow (Rembrandt-like portraits).
- Studio softbox: key at 45°, fill opposite at lower power, rim behind; soft diffused highlights with a soft contact shadow (not "no shadows").
- Hard / dramatic: one small direct source, deep shadows, high contrast; chiaroscuro.
- Backlight / rim: subject outlined against a bright background, glow, possible flare.
- Neon / practicals: colored sources in frame, reflections on wet surfaces, mixed color temperature.
- Name the color temperature when it matters: warm ~3200K, daylight ~5600K, cool shade ~7000K.

4. Mood follows from these choices: write the cause (low warm side light, slow shadows) and add the mood word last, never instead.
