Contrast levels: how far an idea departs from what the viewer expects. They are a tool for ideation, not a setting.

Levels
- 0 none: the idea as expected. A woman has coffee in a cafe.
- 1 unexpected situation: what happens, or who or what is there, is out of place. She has coffee in a laundromat at 3 am, among tumbling clothes.
- 2 unexpected styling or attitude: how the subject looks or behaves clashes with the situation. She sits in a ball gown and sneakers, bored, reading the dryer's manual aloud like a bedtime story.
- 3 feeling: a felt contradiction the viewer senses without it being stated. The gown, the fluorescent hum and her calm read as a private celebration nobody was invited to.

How to use them
1. While you shape the idea (before the plan), judge its level in your head. Do not announce a number unless you are offering variants.
2. If it is 0 or 1, add to your concept message 2-3 variants one level up, one line each, as suggestions the user can take or leave. If it is already 2 or 3, offer nothing.
3. A level touches only the subject and the action: who, what they do, what is around them, how they behave. Never camera, lens, light, texture or capture style: those stay as the user or the preset set them, so any level works with any preset (skate, camcorder, disposable...).
4. When the user asks ("súbelo a nivel 2", "más contraste", "bájalo"), rewrite that part of the prompts and say in one line what changed. Keep identity, product and every user constraint.
5. If the user ignores the suggestion, proceed with the original idea. Do not ask again and do not use a questions card only for this.
6. Raise one level at a time. Level 3 needs a subject that can carry it; if the idea cannot, say so and stay at 2.
