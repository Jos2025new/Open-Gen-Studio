Architectural visualization — stills (exteriors, interiors, the same building from several views)

1. Building sheet first (one identity)
- Before any prompt, write one short building sheet: type (house, apartment, office, pavilion), style (minimalist, brutalist, biophilic, Scandinavian, mid-century…), massing (storeys, roof shape, cantilevers), facade materials (board-formed concrete, walnut slats, travertine, tempered glass, black steel frames), openings (floor-to-ceiling glazing, clerestory), landscape (garden, pool, native grasses).
- Repeat it word for word in every view of the same project and put the approved render in the refs of every later view and clip, so the architecture stays the same (save it to the library only if the user asks). With the user's image, the image is the identity: never re-describe what it shows.

2. Light and atmosphere (one condition per image)
- Name the condition and its direction: golden hour low sun from the left, overcast soft daylight, blue hour with warm interior glow, night with wet reflective ground, morning mist. Give interior light a source: skylight, window wall, cove lighting, pendant.
- Color temperature when it matters: warm interiors 2700–3000K against cool dusk sky.

3. Camera (architectural photography rules)
- Verticals stay vertical: "tilt-shift lens, two-point perspective, straight vertical lines". No dutch angles unless asked.
- Exteriors: eye level 1.6 m or low 0.5 m to add scale; 24–35 mm; three-quarter corner view shows two facades. Aerial or drone only when asked or for site context.
- Interiors: camera height 1.2–1.5 m, 16–24 mm, one-point perspective toward the main window or a corner view; never fisheye distortion.
- Name the composition: building on the right two thirds, foreground planting, leading lines of a path.

4. Scale and life (sparingly)
- A few people at scale, slightly blurred, never the subject; one parked car, furniture that fits the style. Too much entourage hides the architecture.

5. Formats
- Hero exterior 16:9 or 3:2; catalogue/vertical interiors 3:4 or 4:5; social 4:5 or 9:16. Resolution follows the chosen quality; ask only when the destination is unknown and matters.

6. Iterating on an approved render
- Edits change one thing and keep the rest: "change only the facade cladding to dark timber; keep massing, openings, camera, light and landscape". Relight for time of day; Reframe to widen; Upscale last.

7. Avoid
- Bent or leaning verticals, impossible structure (floating slabs without support), windows that change count between views, mixed styles in one building, text or watermarks, oversaturated HDR skies, empty sterile interiors unless asked.
